Blue-Tongue Skink Obesity: Reading Fat Reserves in the Tail Before the Liver

A skink is meant to be broad across the back. It is not meant to be broad at the base of the tail, and that is where the fat that overwhelms the liver is stored.

An adult Tiliqua scincoides in lean condition weighs roughly 400-550 g at 25-30 cm snout-to-vent length, and a blue tongue sitting 20 % above its own spring weight is carrying that surplus inside the coelomic cavity, not just under the skin. The coelomic fat bodies that make the animal look plump are the same tissue that infiltrates the liver. The scale is not a cosmetic instrument; it is a window on organ health.

The skink that taught me to weigh monthly

In January 2021 I brought home a two-year-old female, Bracken, fed three times a week on cat food and banana. On the kitchen scale she read 680 g. I cut her to one meal a week and left it there. Six weeks later she stopped eating — not a refusal, she simply walked away from food she had taken happily for months. Her abdomen was still plump but soft and doughy rather than firm. The blood panel came back with bile acids at 90 µmol/L, well above reference, and the ultrasound showed an enlarged, echogenic liver: early hepatic lipidosis. The bill was £340 for panel, scan and a course of silymarin. I had let her get fat, then starved her too fast, and the fat came out of storage faster than the liver could process it.

Body condition scoring: the numbers that matter

Run your thumb and forefinger down the spine and between the ribs: you should feel vertebrae and ribs without seeing them. Then feel the tail base, the thickest part just past the vent — in a healthy adult roughly as wide as the neck and reasonably firm. Blue tongues store fat in the coelomic fat bodies and the tail, and the tail reads first, before the liver is involved.

I score on five points. Use it at every weigh-in. 1 emaciated: spine and ribs sharp, tail base flat, hips protruding. 2 lean: ribs easily felt, tail base slightly narrower than the neck. 3 ideal: ribs palpable under a thin layer of muscle, tail base equal to neck width, belly gently rounded but not spilling sideways when the animal lies flat. 4 overweight: ribs difficult to feel, tail base wider than the neck, small skin folds at the joints. 5 obese: no ribs felt at all, tail base bulbous, belly visibly sagging, folds at the flanks and under the throat.

On the scale, a score of 3 in a mature animal sits at roughly 400-550 g, a 4 usually at 550-650 g, a 5 over 650 g. Those are ranges, not law — sex and body length shift them — but they give you a starting point. Weigh monthly, same time of day, before feeding, on a scale that reads to 0.1 g.

How fat in the wrong place becomes a failing liver

Surplus energy is stored as triglycerides in those coelomic fat bodies, which are not inert: they release inflammatory signals that damage liver tissue. The liver also accumulates fat directly. Hepatocytes — the liver's working cells — swell with lipid droplets, compressing the small bile ducts and the blood vessels running through the organ. A liver crowded with fat cannot convert and detoxify as it should.

The second hit is speed. Put a fat blue tongue on a crash diet and the body mobilises fat faster than the liver can process it, and the liver becomes the site of the crisis. That is what happened with Bracken. Clinically you see an animal that stops eating, goes lethargic, and sometimes develops a yellowish tinge at the mouth membranes. A suspect animal needs blood biochemistry including bile acids, plus ultrasound — not a change of food. High-fat diets, cat food above all, are the usual history, and I treat any score-4 or score-5 skink as a suspect until a vet says otherwise.

Feeding for condition: the numbers to run

Adults eat twice a week, each meal sized to roughly the volume of the animal's own skull — for a 450 g adult, 40-50 g, removed after 30 minutes. The staple is a prepared blue-tongue or omnivore diet, not cat food: cat food is calorie-dense and often close to 1:1 calcium-to-phosphorus, where a blue tongue wants roughly 1.5:1 to 2:1. I rotate cooked lean turkey mince, gut-loaded Dubia roaches or snails (rich in calcium, low in fat), and a little dark leafy green, dusting insect and meat meals with one pinch of fine calcium powder with D3 per meal.

Juveniles are a different animal. Growing fast, they take four meals a week of 20-30 g, weighted toward insects and lean protein, and they should stay lean at score 2-3 — never 4. Rates of change, and the husbandry half of the equation

Safe loss is 5-10 % of body weight per month, so a 650 g animal sheds 30-65 g in month one. Weigh weekly during a reduction, before feeding, and write it down. Lose more than 10 % and increase the portion slightly; lose nothing for three weeks and cut another 10 %.

Condition is an output of husbandry, not just food. An adult needs a basking spot of 35-40 °C measured with an infrared thermometer at the surface the animal actually sits on, a cool end at 22-25 °C, and a photoperiod of 10-12 hours in winter, 12-14 in summer. A basking spot at 42 °C produces an animal that parks under the lamp and stops moving; too cool a spot means poor digestion and a skink that eats less than you think. Minimum enclosure for an adult is 120 × 60 × 60 cm, with 10-15 cm of a 60:40 soil-and-playsand mix that holds a burrow — dry at the surface, faintly damp beneath. A blue tongue that can dig is a blue tongue that moves.

Telling a winter fast from a crisis

A healthy adult may go off food for 4-6 weeks over winter as temperatures and light hours drop. That is normal. What is not normal is a refusal that follows a fat period, or one that starts while the animal is already score 4 or 5. The tell is the body: still plump, but soft and doughy rather than firm. There is no home test for liver function; bile acids above 40 µmol/L are suggestive and above 80 µmol/L concerning. If your fat skink stops eating, the next appointment is an exotics vet. What you can control at home is the log — a 0.1 g scale and a notebook, weighed weekly through any reduction, so you know the trend before the behaviour changes.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can a fat blue tongue lose weight safely?

5-10 % of body weight per month. A 650 g animal should lose 30-65 g in the first month and no more. Faster than that and the liver is handling too much fat mobilisation at once, which is how a diet turns into lipidosis. Weigh weekly and adjust portions in 10 % steps.

Is a fat blue tongue always overfed?

No. A basking spot below 35 °C slows digestion and activity, so a normally fed animal can still gain. But most score-4 and score-5 animals I have seen were on cat food or large fruit portions. Check the diet first, then the temperatures, then the portion size.

Can I score body condition at home without a vet?

Yes — feel the ribs and tail base, weigh monthly, record the score. But a 5, or any 4 that stops eating, needs a veterinary work-up: blood biochemistry and ultrasound. Once the liver is involved you are managing pathology, not just trimming a ration. Scoring is a screen, not a diagnosis.
