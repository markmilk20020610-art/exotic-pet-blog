Do Leopard Geckos Need UVB Light or Just Heat Mats? The Ultimate Lighting Guide

You've set up the perfect tank. The hides are in place, the water dish is clean, and your new leopard gecko is exploring its new home. But then you jump onto a forum or a Facebook group, and suddenly you're hit with conflicting advice: "Leopard geckos are nocturnal, they don't need UVB!" versus "Heat mats are outdated and dangerous—you need a UVB bulb!"

It's one of the most debated topics in the reptile hobby, and it can be incredibly confusing for a new keeper. If you get it wrong, you risk your gecko developing Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD) or suffering from thermal burns. If you get it right, you'll have a thriving, active gecko with strong bones and vibrant colors.

So, let's settle the debate. Do leopard geckos need UVB light or just heat mats? The short answer is: They benefit significantly from UVB, but a heat mat is often the most practical primary heat source. However, the best setup actually involves a combination of both. Let's break down exactly how to build the perfect habitat.

The Great Debate: UVB vs. Heat Mats Explained

To understand what your gecko needs, you first have to understand their natural history. Leopard geckos are crepuscular, meaning they are most active during dawn and dusk. In the wild, they are exposed to low levels of UVB during these twilight hours, and they can also seek out sunlight if needed.

Why the "No UVB" Myth Exists

For decades, the hobby standard was to keep leopard geckos without UVB, relying entirely on calcium and vitamin D3 supplements. Because they are nocturnal, it was assumed they didn't need light. While it is true that they can survive without UVB if supplemented perfectly, "surviving" is not the same as "thriving."

The Case for Heat Mats

Leopard geckos require a belly heat source to digest their food properly. In the wild, they thermoregulate by pressing their bellies against warm rocks that have absorbed the sun's heat. A heat mat attached to the underside of the tank perfectly mimics this, providing the necessary 24/7 heat gradient without drying out the air.

The Case for UVB

Recent studies have shown that even crepuscular reptiles benefit from UVB exposure. UVB allows the gecko's body to synthesize Vitamin D3 naturally, which is essential for absorbing calcium. This natural process is far more efficient than relying solely on dusted supplements, leading to stronger bones, better immune systems, and more natural behaviors.

How to Set Up the Perfect Lighting and Heating System

You don't have to choose between UVB and heat mats—the ideal setup uses both to create a microclimate that mimics the wild. Here is a step-by-step guide to setting it up correctly.

  • Step 1: Install the Heat Mat: Place a heat mat (connected to a thermostat) under one-third of the tank. This creates a "hot spot" of about 88–92°F (31–33°C) on the floor. This should remain on 24/7.
  • Step 2: Add the UVB Light: Use a low-output UVB bulb (typically 2%–5% or a T5 HO 5.0). Place this on the same side as the heat mat. The UVB should be on a timer for 10–12 hours a day, mimicking the sun.
  • Step 3: Create a Gradient: Ensure the opposite side of the tank remains cool (75–80°F). Your gecko needs to be able to escape the heat and light to cool down.
  • Step 4: Provide Cover: Leopard geckos are prey animals. Ensure there are plenty of hides and foliage so they can move around under the UVB without feeling exposed.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Can I use a red or blue night light for my leopard gecko?

No. Leopard geckos can see red and blue light, and it disrupts their natural day/night cycle. If you need heat at night, stick to a heat mat or a Ceramic Heat Emitter (CHE) which produces zero light. Your tank should be dark at night.

2. What happens if I use UVB without a thermostat on my heat mat?

This is a dangerous combination. UVB bulbs can generate intense heat if placed too close to the basking spot. Always use a thermostat with any heat source to prevent your gecko from suffering thermal burns. The UVB fixture should be mounted on the mesh lid, not inside the tank where the gecko can climb on it.

3. Do I still need to dust with calcium if I use UVB?

Yes, but you can adjust the frequency. With UVB, your gecko produces its own Vitamin D3. You should still provide a small dish of calcium powder without D3 in the tank at all times. Dusting feeders with a multivitamin is still recommended once a week, but you can reduce the use of D3 supplements to avoid overdose.
